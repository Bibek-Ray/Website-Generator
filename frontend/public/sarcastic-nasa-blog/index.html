Blog Posts (Chronological)

NASA Announces New Mission: Finding Alien Tax Forms

October 26, 2023 by Space Cadet Snarkington

In a stunning display of bureaucratic ambition, NASA has unveiled its latest project: a deep-space probe designed to locate and analyze alien tax returns. 'We believe the universe is teeming with unreported income,' said a NASA spokesperson, adjusting their pocket protector. 'And we're just the agency to audit it.' Prepare for years of budget overruns and the inevitable alien version of the IRS.

The James Webb Telescope: Finally, We Can See...More Questions

October 19, 2023 by Cosmic Curmudgeon

The James Webb Telescope continues to beam back images so stunning, they make us question everything we thought we knew – except for the fact that space is REALLY far away. Scientists are now arguing about whether the blurry blobs are galaxies, or just cosmic dust bunnies. Either way, the bill for the dust bunnies is astronomical.

NASA's New Astronaut Training: Surviving PowerPoint Presentations

October 12, 2023 by Orbiting Cynic

Forget zero-G simulations and rocket science! The future of space travel, according to NASA, lies in mastering the art of the PowerPoint presentation. Astronauts now spend more time perfecting bullet points than they do practicing spacewalks. 'We need to prepare them for the real challenges of space,' a NASA trainer explained, 'like the dreaded 'death by slideshow' from mission control.'

Mars Rover Finds...Rocks. Again.

October 5, 2023 by Astro-Skeptic

Another day, another Martian rock. The Mars rover, in its tireless quest for something, anything, remotely interesting, has once again sent back images of...well, rocks. 'We're still hopeful we'll find something,' mumbled a geologist, staring forlornly at a grainy photo of a reddish pebble. 'Maybe a slightly different shaped rock?' The search continues (and the budget keeps growing).

About Us (Because Apparently, You Care)

We're not exactly rocket scientists (thankfully, since NASA seems to have enough of those). We're just a bunch of folks who find the whole space thing... well, let's just say it's endlessly entertaining.

We're here to dissect NASA's latest endeavors with the wit and cynicism they so richly deserve. Expect witty remarks, snarky observations, and the occasional conspiracy theory (mostly because they're fun, not because we actually believe them... probably).

So, buckle up, buttercups. It's going to be a bumpy ride through the cosmos of questionable decisions, taxpayer-funded extravaganzas, and the eternal quest to find out if there's life... and whether or not it's as weird as we are.

Disclaimer: We love space. We just love making fun of it a little more. And yes, we know it's hard. That's why we find it so funny.

Astronauts: They're Just Like Us (Except, You Know, Space)

The Right Stuff (And a Really Good Dentist)

Think you've got what it takes to be an astronaut? Well, besides years of training, a Ph.D. in rocket science, and the ability to tolerate zero gravity, you'll also need perfect teeth. Seriously, NASA is weird about dental hygiene. Probably because they can't get a decent dentist in orbit.

Space Food: Gourmet Cuisine or Vomit Comet?

Forget Michelin stars. Astronauts dine on freeze-dried, pre-packaged 'delights' that taste suspiciously like cardboard. But hey, at least they have the option of adding water! (Pro Tip: Always add *slightly* less water than recommended.)

The Ultimate Selfie Game

Astronauts are basically professional selfie-takers. They've got the best backdrop imaginable – Earth! – and they're not afraid to use it. Just try not to get too jealous of their Instagram feeds. (We are.)

Zero Gravity, Maximum Awkwardness

Floating in space might *look* cool, but imagine trying to eat, sleep, or, you know, *go* to the bathroom while weightless. It's a recipe for maximum awkwardness and a whole lot of floating...stuff.

Returning to Earth: The Ultimate Hangover

After months in space, coming back to Earth is like experiencing the world's worst hangover, mixed with motion sickness, and the existential dread of realizing you're now used to floating. Welcome home, space cadets!
